Vote Yes on Amendment 3: Taxpayers Deserve Relief—and Government Must Set Priorities

By John Watts, Editor · The Heron Report  |  October 20, 2026
Florida Amendment 3 — homestead exemption increase on the 2026 ballot.

Florida families have been told to tighten their belts. Seniors living on fixed incomes must stretch their checks to cover groceries, insurance, medicine and the cost of keeping their homes.

Now that government faces pressure to practice the same fiscal discipline, the objections are getting louder.

My position is simple: taxpayers deserve relief, and elected officials must explain how they will protect essential services while living within a more disciplined budget.

Florida's Amendment 3 would increase the qualifying homestead exemption for non-school property taxes to $150,000 in 2027 and $250,000 in 2028. School property taxes would remain. It is meaningful relief, although it is not an immediate end to every homeowner's tax bill.

For seniors, a house increasing in value does not mean their retirement income increases with it. A homeowner should not have to sell the place they worked decades to pay for simply because the cost of staying there keeps climbing.

That deserves as much attention as government's concerns about its own revenue.

Public safety should always have been a priority

Whenever tax relief comes up, police officers and firefighters become central to the argument against it.

But voters should ask a fair question: How well were those services supported before tax relief was proposed?

Where firefighters have struggled for equipment, training or staffing, officials should explain those decisions. Where law enforcement has unmet needs, taxpayers deserve to see what was requested, what was funded and what took priority instead.

Invoking first responders during a tax debate does not answer questions about past spending choices.

I support strong law enforcement, properly equipped firefighters and reliable emergency response. That is why I believe these services should be at the front of the budget discussion every year.

Show taxpayers the books

Reduced revenue creates real challenges. Amendment 3 does not guarantee that waste alone will cover the difference, and supporters should be honest about that.

But before officials propose cutting frontline services, they should publicly examine administrative costs, contracts, consulting expenses, overlapping functions and projects that can wait.

Explain the alternatives. Publish the numbers. Let residents see the choices.

Fiscal responsibility also means resisting the temptation to replace property-tax savings with a collection of higher fees. Relief should reach household budgets.

Homeownership should provide stability

Working families and retirees need room to breathe. Keeping more of their money can help cover repairs, insurance and everyday necessities.

Government's responsibility is to serve those residents while protecting the services they depend on. That requires planning, clear priorities and accountability.

My vote is yes on Amendment 3 because homeowners deserve meaningful relief—and because asking government to justify its spending is a reasonable demand.

Protect our seniors. Support our first responders. Put essential services first. Make every tax dollar count.
