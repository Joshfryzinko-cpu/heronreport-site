EIA: Winter Energy Costs to Vary Widely — Natural Gas Down, Electricity Up

The U.S. Energy Information Administration’s winter outlook projects a “mixed picture” for American households: natural gas heating costs are expected to fall due to high inventories and increased domestic production, while electricity prices continue their upward trend. Crude oil markets remain volatile as Middle East tensions and OPEC production decisions weigh on global supply. The EIA report is a key indicator used by policymakers and energy traders.

Federal Reserve Signals Another Rate Hike as Inflation Persists

Federal Reserve officials telegraphed a likely interest rate increase before year’s end, citing persistent inflation that has kept prices above the Fed’s 2 percent target. The announcement sent markets lower and reignited debate over the Fed’s independence from the White House, as President Trump has publicly pressured the central bank to lower rates. Higher borrowing costs continue to squeeze American homebuyers, small businesses, and farmers.

Global Military Spending to Hit $3.29 Trillion in 2026 as Conflicts Escalate

The Stockholm International Peace Research Institute projects global defense spending will reach a record $3.29 trillion in 2026, driven by the Russia-Ukraine war, Middle East conflicts, and China’s military buildup. The United States remains the world’s largest defense spender. NATO allies have increased contributions following Trump’s pressure campaign, though critics argue the spending surge raises risks of further escalation and crowds out domestic priorities.
