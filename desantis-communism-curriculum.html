Governor DeSantis Makes History of Communism Required Education in Florida Public Schools

By John Watts, Editor | The Heron Report  ·  November 17, 2026
Governor Ron DeSantis has expanded Florida's public school curriculum to require instruction on the history of communism.

FLORIDA — Governor Ron DeSantis has expanded Florida’s public school curriculum, requiring students to learn about the history of communism, the governments that embraced it, and the consequences those governments had on millions of people around the world.

The new education requirements are being implemented during the 2026–2027 school year, following legislation signed by DeSantis on April 17, 2024.

Under Senate Bill 1264, Florida public schools must provide age-appropriate instruction covering communist movements, political revolutions, government oppression, and the historical conflicts between communist systems and American democratic principles.

What Will Students Learn?

New curriculum standards take effect in Florida classrooms for grades 6 through 12.

The law establishes several required areas of instruction, including:

  • The Soviet Union: The Russian Revolution, Joseph Stalin’s rule, political persecution, and the suppression of individual freedoms.
  • Communist China: Mao Zedong’s leadership, the Cultural Revolution, and the mass suffering associated with communist government policies.
  • Cuba: Fidel Castro’s rise to power, the Cuban Revolution, restrictions on political freedoms, and the influence of communism throughout Latin America.
  • Communist Movements in America: The history of communist organizations in the United States, their activities, and their influence during the 20th century.
  • Government and Individual Rights: Comparisons between communist and totalitarian political systems and the constitutional principles of the United States.

The curriculum also examines economic and political conditions that contributed to communist revolutions and how those movements affected international relations.

Why Did Florida Pass This Law?

DeSantis and supporters of the legislation argue that younger generations need a stronger understanding of historical events involving communist governments.

Florida has a particularly significant connection to this history through its Cuban American population, including families who fled Cuba following Fidel Castro’s rise to power.

The governor previously signed legislation in 2022 establishing November 7 as Victims of Communism Day, requiring schools to provide instruction recognizing people who suffered under communist regimes.

The 2024 legislation expands that earlier requirement into broader historical instruction.

New Standards Now in Effect

On November 13, 2025, the Florida State Board of Education approved new History of Communism standards.

The standards are being implemented during the current school year, including new benchmarks for grades 6 through 12.

In September 2026, DeSantis publicly celebrated the implementation, emphasizing the importance of teaching students about government power, individual rights, and the historical consequences of communist rule.

Not Everyone Agrees on the Curriculum

While supporters believe the requirements will strengthen historical knowledge and civic education, some historians and educators have questioned how certain subjects are presented.

Particular concerns involve the treatment of McCarthyism, Cold War investigations, and the potential for political bias in classroom instruction.

Critics argue that students should learn about communist atrocities while also examining historical controversies involving anti-communist investigations and restrictions on civil liberties in the United States.

What Does This Mean for Local Schools?

Public school districts throughout Florida, including Gulf, Franklin, and Bay counties, are subject to the statewide instructional requirements.

The new material is intended to become part of students’ broader social studies education rather than requiring a separate, standalone class on communism.

For Florida families, the changes mean students will receive more structured instruction on political ideology, historical conflicts, government authority, and constitutional freedoms.
