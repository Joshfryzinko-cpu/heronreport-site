Politics Can Divide Us. Protecting St. Joseph Bay Should Unite Us.

By John Watts, Editor | The Heron Report  |  October 25, 2026
Dead fish blanketing the water beside marina docks in Port St. Joe. — Photo: The Heron Report

PORT ST. JOE — A photograph showing a dense blanket of dead fish beside marina docks in Port St. Joe is difficult to look at. It should also be difficult to ignore.

The photograph alone cannot establish what killed the fish or how widespread the problem is. Those answers require investigation and water testing. But protecting the water that defines this community should be something everyone can get behind.

"Politics are one thing. The health of St. Joseph Bay is something we all have a stake in."

Republicans, Democrats, and independents fish these waters. Their children swim and boat here. Local businesses depend on people wanting to visit, explore, and enjoy our coast.

For Port St. Joe, healthy water is part of our livelihood, our identity, and the future we leave our children.

The same scene after dark — fish kill at the marina docks, Port St. Joe. — Photo: The Heron Report

When dead fish collect along our waterfront, residents deserve clear answers. What testing has been done? What do the results show? What remains unknown? What response is needed?

Asking those questions should bring people together. We can insist on a thorough investigation without jumping to conclusions about the cause or assigning blame before the evidence is available.

Local officials, environmental agencies, fishermen, boaters, and business owners should work toward the same goal: understanding what happened and protecting our waterways. Findings should be shared openly, with clear explanations and updates as more information becomes available.

Whatever the investigation determines, the public deserves a response grounded in evidence and a commitment to protecting the bay.

We will continue to disagree about elections, taxes, and government spending. But surely we can agree that dead fish covering the water beside our docks deserve attention — and that clean, healthy water is worth working together to protect.

St. Joseph Bay belongs to no political party. Its future matters to all of us.
