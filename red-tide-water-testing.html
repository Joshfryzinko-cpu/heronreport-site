Gulf County Approves Red Tide Water Testing for St. Joseph Bay

By John Watts, Editor | The Heron Report  ·  October 28, 2026
Gulf County commissioners approved water testing for St. Joseph Bay at Tuesday's meeting. — Photo: The Heron Report

PORT ST. JOE — Gulf County commissioners are moving forward with water testing aimed at identifying pollution that may be contributing to the red tide affecting St. Joseph Bay and helping guide a plan of action.

According to the county's October 28 release, commissioners approved funding Tuesday for nutrient and bacteria sampling in partnership with the St. Andrew and St. Joseph Bays Estuary Program.

Sampling is scheduled for Thursday, October 29, at six locations around the bay. Testing will measure nitrogen and phosphorus — nutrients that can fuel algae blooms — and check for bacteria that can help indicate whether pollution is coming from human sources or cattle.

The county says commissioners will review the results as soon as they become available and use those findings to guide steps to reduce pollution that can worsen blooms and protect the bay's health.

The testing is an investigative step. The release does not identify a confirmed pollution source or establish what caused the current bloom.

Red Tide Health Alerts

DOH-Gulf has issued health alerts for the following locations:

• Cape Palms Park — Gulf side of Cape San Blas
• Clifford G. Sims Memorial Park
• Country Club Road in Simmons Bayou

Residents with health questions can call 850-227-1276.

For residents concerned about the bay, this effort moves the discussion toward measurable findings and decisions informed by testing.

Source: Gulf County Board of County Commissioners, October 28, 2026, press release.
