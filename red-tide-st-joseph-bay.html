Dead Fish Raise Alarm as FWC Confirms Red Tide in St. Joseph Bay

By John Watts, Editor | The Heron Report  |  October 24, 2026
FWC statewide Karenia brevis sampling map, Oct. 15–22, 2026. Orange dots in Gulf County indicate medium bloom-level concentrations in St. Joseph Bay. — Florida Fish and Wildlife Conservation Commission

⚠️ Active Red Tide Alert — St. Joseph Bay. FWC updates its sampling map daily at 5 p.m. Next statewide report: October 30. Track conditions at myfwc.com.

PORT ST. JOE, Fla. — Reports of dead marine life in St. Joseph Bay are raising concern over the health of one of Gulf County's most valued natural resources, as state wildlife officials confirm red tide has reached bloom concentrations.

The Florida Fish and Wildlife Conservation Commission, or FWC, reported October 23 that two samples collected in St. Joseph Bay contained bloom-level concentrations of Karenia brevis, the organism responsible for Florida red tide. These were the first samples to reach that threshold in Florida this season. (myfwc.com)

FWC also received several reports of fish kills suspected to be related to red tide in the bay. Respiratory irritation was reported in the same area. Although those observations point to red tide as a possible cause, the agency's report does not establish the cause of every animal death or document the full extent of losses. (myfwc.com)

For a community closely connected to its coastal waters, the findings raise pressing questions: How far does the bloom extend, how much marine life has been affected, and will conditions worsen?

Samples collected in Gulf County ranged from low to medium concentrations. Elsewhere, FWC detected only background levels in Bay County and offshore of Lee County, making St. Joseph Bay the location of the bloom-level detections identified in the latest statewide report. (myfwc.com)

FWC sampling map Oct. 15–22, 2026. Orange = medium bloom (100,000–1,000,000 cells/liter). Gulf County and Franklin County show the only bloom-level readings in Florida this period.

The findings warrant close attention, but they do not establish pollution, a discharge, or wrongdoing as the source of the bloom. Any explanation of what triggered this event will require additional evidence.

Residents can follow conditions through FWC's daily sampling map, updated at 5 p.m. The agency's next scheduled statewide report is October 30. For St. Joseph Bay, continued sampling will be critical to understanding whether the bloom is spreading or subsiding. (myfwc.com)
