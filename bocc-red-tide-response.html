'I've Never Seen It Like This': Gulf County Commissioners Unite Behind St. Joseph Bay

A lifelong Gulf County resident spoke from the heart. His fellow commissioners listened — and the board left the October 27 meeting committed to a plan.

By John Watts, Editor | The Heron Report  ·  October 27, 2026
Gulf County commissioners addressed the red tide crisis at Monday's regular meeting in Port St. Joe. — Photo: The Heron Report

PORT ST. JOE — When the October 27 Gulf County Board of County Commissioners meeting turned to red tide, Commissioner Phil McCroan did not speak in talking points. He spoke as someone who grew up on these shores.

"I've lived here since I was a child," McCroan told the board. "I have never seen a red tide spike like this before."

"I've lived here since I was a child. I have never seen a red tide spike like this before."
— Commissioner Phil McCroan, Oct. 27, 2026

Those words landed. McCroan has watched St. Joseph Bay through decades of seasonal changes, fishing seasons good and bad, storms that reshaped the coastline, and summers that drew visitors from across the country. For him to say this red tide event stands apart from anything in his memory carries real weight.

Commissioner Christopher "Spike" McLemore was not content to simply acknowledge the problem. He pushed the board toward action, calling for a coordinated response plan — a clear, organized approach to protecting the bay, communicating with residents, and mobilizing resources before conditions worsen.

The call resonated. The board concluded its discussion with a shared commitment to move forward together on a response. In a county where healthy water is not a luxury but a livelihood — where fishing families, local businesses, tourism, and the daily quality of life all flow from the health of St. Joseph Bay — that commitment matters.

Red tide is not a political issue. It does not care which party a commissioner belongs to, which district they represent, or how they voted on the last budget. What it does is test whether the people elected to lead a community will rise to meet a challenge that touches everyone.

On Monday, they did.

McCroan's personal connection to the bay gives his concern authenticity that no prepared statement could match. McLemore's push for a plan gives that concern a direction. Together, they reflect what residents of Gulf County expect from their elected leaders when something is wrong with the water that defines this place.

The Heron Report will continue to follow the board's response as the plan develops and the FWC's next statewide red tide report arrives on October 30.
