Florida Election Goes Ahead as Flooding Forces Polling Places to Move in Eight Counties

By The Heron Report, Staff  ·  November 3, 2026
Illustration: The Heron Report

Florida’s midterm election went ahead Tuesday after heavy rain flooded roads, stranded residents and forced election officials to move polling places in eight counties. Gov. Ron DeSantis declared a state of emergency in seven counties Monday and authorized election officials to relocate sites that were unsafe or inaccessible. Voting continued on schedule.

In Brevard County, the Melbourne area recorded more than 17 inches of rain in the 24 hours ending Monday afternoon, while Grant-Valkaria recorded 23.6 inches, according to Central Florida Public Media. Some Brevard residents found themselves unable to leave their neighborhoods because roads were underwater or washed out. In Broward County, residents of Davie’s Paradise Village told CBS Miami that floodwater kept some neighbors from getting out to vote. Election officials redirected voters there to Tree Tops Park.

Of the eight counties that moved polling places, six voted Republican and two voted Democratic in the 2024 presidential election. Democrat Kamala Harris carried Broward by about 17 percentage points and Palm Beach by less than one percentage point.

The governor’s emergency order covered Brevard, DeSoto, Hardee, Highlands, Indian River, Martin and St. Lucie. Four of the eight counties that moved polling places were outside that declaration: Broward, Martin, Okeechobee and Palm Beach.

Replacement polling places gave voters with a passable route somewhere to cast a ballot. In neighborhoods where floodwater blocked the way out, residents still had to wait for a safe route. How many voters ultimately missed the election because of flooding remains unclear.
