Miami-Dade Correctional Officer Federally Charged in Drug Smuggling and Gambling Ring Inside County Jails

ⓘ Agency seal used — no confirmed photo of this event is used here.

Renel Troutman, a Miami-Dade County corrections officer, was federally charged Sept. 30, 2026 with conspiracy to possess narcotics with intent to distribute, Hobbs Act extortion under color of official right, and racketeering promotion. Federal investigators say Troutman was a member of a criminal enterprise that smuggled drugs into Miami-Dade jail facilities and facilitated illegal inmate gambling operations, including bets placed through the Hard Rock sportsbook. Two Women Sue Former Florida Rep. Matt Gaetz in Federal Court, Alleging Sex Trafficking and Drug-Fueled Exploitation

Two women filed a federal civil lawsuit Oct. 15, 2026 in the U.S. District Court for the Middle District of Florida against former Congressman Matt Gaetz, alleging he participated in a sex-trafficking scheme involving drugs, paid sex, and interstate travel. The plaintiffs say they were in their early 20s in 2017 when Joel Greenberg — now serving 11 years in federal prison for sex trafficking and wire fraud — recruited and introduced them to Gaetz. The lawsuit draws on the bipartisan House Ethics Committee report, which found “substantial evidence” Gaetz had paid for sex and used illicit drugs while in office. Gaetz denies the allegations and says he will confront his accusers in court. This is a civil lawsuit. No criminal charges have been filed against Gaetz in this matter.

📍 Tallahassee, FL

DeSantis, AG Uthmeier Announce 20 Arrests for Election Fraud — Foreign Nationals and Felons Charged with Illegal Voting

Florida Gov. Ron DeSantis and Attorney General James Uthmeier announced Sept. 24, 2026 the arrests of 20 people for election fraud, the latest enforcement action by the Office of Election Crimes and Security. Uthmeier said most suspects were foreign nationals who fraudulently registered to vote and then cast ballots — both felonies — while approximately one-fifth were convicted felons who had lost their voting rights. Twelve suspects were already in custody with others still being located. The announcement marks more than 100 prosecutions by Florida’s election integrity office since it was established in 2022. DeSantis credited the office’s formation with significantly strengthening accountability in Florida elections. All defendants are presumed innocent until proven guilty in court.

Federal Judge Rules Musk, Noem Spread “Entirely False Information” to Justify Firing FEMA CFO

ⓘ Agency seal used — no confirmed photo of this event is used here.

U.S. District Judge William Fitzpatrick ruled Oct. 13 that top Trump administration officials — including Elon Musk and former DHS Secretary Kristi Noem — spread “entirely false information” to justify firing FEMA Chief Financial Officer Mary Comans. Comans was dismissed in February 2025 amid claims she had approved millions in hotel rooms for undocumented immigrants. Judge Fitzpatrick found the spending was approved by others and called the actions “outrageous government conduct,” writing that officials “knowingly spreading false information” turned government power against “a singular employee with an impeccable record of public service.” Comans was cleared of all wrongdoing.

📍 Tallahassee, FL

Florida Appeals Court Expunges Hope Florida Grand Jury Report — Rules It Exceeded Authority and Violated Confidentiality Law

Florida’s First District Court of Appeal ordered a Leon County grand jury’s 116-page Hope Florida presentment to be fully expunged, ruling the panel exceeded its authority and that state confidentiality laws were violated when the report was leaked. The grand jury had alleged a “sophisticated scheme” to misappropriate $10 million from a Medicaid settlement — money directed to the Hope Florida Foundation — for political purposes, but declined to indict anyone. The appeals court ruled the $10 million was never public money and the transfer was lawful. Gov. DeSantis called the ruling a “judicial equivalent of a death-by-firing squad to the Hope Florida Hoax.” A separate criminal investigation is ongoing by a different state attorney’s office. No charges have been filed.

📍 Washington, D.C.

Supreme Court Agrees to Hear Whether DOGE Must Disclose Records to Watchdog Group

The U.S. Supreme Court agreed Oct. 13 to take up a dispute over whether the Department of Government Efficiency is subject to the Freedom of Information Act. The case, U.S. DOGE Service v. U.S. District Court for the D.C., stems from a FOIA request by Citizens for Responsibility and Ethics in Washington (CREW). The Trump administration has blocked the request, arguing DOGE is a purely advisory body within the Executive Office of the President and therefore not covered by FOIA. A GAO report released in August found DOGE’s “Wall of Receipts” overstated claimed savings, with 96% of grant savings unverifiable and 108 of 264 claimed lease terminations already in process before DOGE existed. Arguments expected in early 2027.
