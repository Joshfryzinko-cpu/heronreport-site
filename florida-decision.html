The Day I Decided to Move to Florida

By John Watts, Editor-in-Chief · The Heron Report  |  October 22, 2026
Portsmouth, Virginia — Confederate monument toppled during the June 10, 2020 demonstration. A man was seriously injured when the statue fell. — AP Photo

In 2020, watching events unfold in Portsmouth, Virginia, I made a decision that would change my life: I wanted to move to Florida.

The turning point was the June 10 demonstration at Portsmouth's Confederate monument. Protesters damaged the monument and pulled down a statue, seriously injuring a man named Chris Green. State Senator Louise Lucas's presence at the demonstration and the controversy surrounding her actions became a focus of public debate. (courthousenews.com)

"I believed elected officials had a responsibility to help prevent a dangerous situation from escalating."

For me, the events raised fundamental questions about leadership, public safety and accountability. I believed elected officials had a responsibility to help prevent a dangerous situation from escalating.

At the time, my friend, Virginia State Senator Amanda Chase, was running for governor in the 2021 election. Another political colleague, attorney Tim Anderson, pursued accountability through a campaign to remove Lucas from office. (vpm.org, whro.org)

Confederate monuments across Virginia became flashpoints during the summer of 2020.

Virginia law makes conspiring to cause a riot, or inciting participants to acts of force or violence, a Class 5 felony. That provision does not require someone to be seriously injured or killed. Whether an individual violated it requires evidence establishing the elements of the offense. (law.lis.virginia.gov)

Police later charged Lucas with conspiracy to commit a felony and damage to the monument. Those charges were dismissed in November 2020; they did not establish that she caused or incited a riot. (whsv.com, whro.org)

Virginia State Senator Louise Lucas. — Virginia General Assembly (official)

Lucas is no stranger to legal controversy. On October 22, 2026, federal authorities announced an indictment accusing her of racketeering conspiracy, marijuana distribution conspiracy, money laundering conspiracy and corruption involving alleged bribery. Prosecutors allege she used her public office to benefit a family-run marijuana enterprise. Lucas denies wrongdoing, and the charges remain allegations, not convictions. (justice.gov, axios.com)

The legal record matters. So does my personal reaction to what unfolded in 2020: it profoundly damaged my confidence in Virginia's political leadership.

That was the day I decided to build a future in Florida.

Today, as editor-in-chief of The Heron Report, I carry that experience with me. It helps explain why public accountability matters so much to me — and why I believe every community deserves careful scrutiny of the people entrusted to lead it.

Editor's Commentary reflects the views of the author. The federal charges against Louise Lucas are allegations only; she has denied wrongdoing and is presumed innocent unless convicted in a court of law.
