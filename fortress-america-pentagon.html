Pentagon Preparing Military Bases for 90 Days Without Outside Support

By John Watts, Editor | The Heron Report  |  October 26, 2026
Senior military and civilian leaders at the Pentagon. The FORTRESS America initiative aims to give major U.S. bases 90 days of independent operating capability. — U.S. Department of Defense

WASHINGTON — What happens when the military installations responsible for defending America lose the civilian services that keep them running?

The Pentagon is developing a plan intended to give major military bases in the continental United States the ability to sustain essential operations independently for at least 90 days, according to reporting by Anadolu Agency citing documents reviewed by Fox News Digital. The reported standard includes electricity, water, fuel and communications during a major disruption. (aa.com.tr)

The effort falls under FORTRESS America, a broader initiative announced in September to strengthen military infrastructure and reduce dependence on vulnerable supply networks. An official Army account of the announcement describes the program as an effort to move military basing and infrastructure toward greater independence. (army.mil)

For readers unfamiliar with the military term, "sustainment" means keeping a mission functioning over time. Having troops, aircraft and equipment available is only part of the equation. They also need dependable utilities, maintenance, supplies and communications.

The central question is whether those necessities would remain available during a prolonged emergency.

Keeping the Mission Running

According to DefenseMirror's account of the reported plan, potential energy measures include small portable nuclear reactors, large battery systems and local electrical networks capable of supporting installations when civilian power supplies are disrupted.

The same reporting describes proposed reserves of ammunition, fuel, medical supplies, spare parts, electronics and critical minerals. It also outlines an effort to establish alternative suppliers so that losing one factory or production route would not halt access to essential components. (defensemirror.com)

That approach would extend preparations beyond the installation fence. Through the Civil Reserve Manufacturing Network, the Pentagon intends to identify commercial manufacturers that could shift production toward defense needs during a crisis. Advance work would include contracts, technical requirements, cybersecurity protections and procedures for converting production. (defensemirror.com)

A Goal, With Questions Still Unanswered

The reported 90-day standard should be understood as a readiness objective. It does not establish that every major installation already has three months of independent operating capability.

International Business Times reported that the Pentagon had not publicly announced a program-wide completion date or total budget. Reaching the goal would require more than installing backup power: bases would also need reliable water, fuel, communications and replacement components throughout an extended disruption. (ibtimes.co.uk)

DefenseScoop separately reported on October 7 that acquisition and sustainment leadership had issued a memorandum directing officials to establish and operationalize a dedicated FORTRESS America program office. (defensescoop.com)

The distinction matters: preparing for a severe contingency is not, by itself, evidence that such an event is imminent.

For the public, the practical question is how progress will be measured. Funding announcements and new equipment can show activity, but the meaningful test is whether installations can maintain their essential missions when outside services are unavailable.

FORTRESS America puts that challenge at the center of military preparedness: defense depends on the systems that keep forces operating, as well as the forces themselves.
