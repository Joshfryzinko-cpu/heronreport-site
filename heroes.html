Heroes of the Week

Recognizing the citizens, journalists, officials, and watchdogs who stood up for transparency, accountability, and truth in Florida and beyond.

Week of October 13, 2026
Electronic Frontier Foundation
Digital Rights Organization
Why They're a Hero

EFF filed a public records demand against Marin County police who illegally shared Flock Safety license plate reader data with out-of-jurisdiction agencies — without legal authorization. Their work exposed how surveillance data flows between police departments with zero public oversight.

"Law enforcement cannot use secretive commercial surveillance networks to share data with whoever they want, whenever they want."
Source: EFF Deeplinks · October 2026
Week of October 13, 2026
ProPublica Investigations
Investigative Journalism
Why They're a Hero

ProPublica documented how DHS used surveillance drones, masked federal agents, and psychological pressure tactics against the Tohono O'odham Nation — an Indigenous tribe on the Arizona border — in retaliation for members speaking to the press about border wall construction.

Reporting that holds the most powerful government in the world accountable to the people it is supposed to serve.
Source: ProPublica · October 2026
Week of October 13, 2026
You — Our Readers
Gulf County, Florida
Why You're a Hero

Every citizen who attends a BOCC meeting, files a public records request, reads the minutes, or asks a hard question at a public meeting is a hero. Gulf County's government only works transparently because people are watching. Keep watching.

"Sunlight is said to be the best of disinfectants." — Justice Louis Brandeis
