Standing With Her Crew: Oklahoma Fire Chief Resigns Alongside Volunteers

By John Watts, Editor  |  The Heron Report  |  October 15, 2026

Leadership carries weight when standing beside your people comes at a personal cost.

In Chouteau, Oklahoma, Fire Chief Amber Williams resigned during an October 13 town council meeting after volunteer firefighters began turning in their radios and announcing their departures. Her decision followed a confrontation over department leadership, staffing and communication, according to KJRH and FOX23 reporting.

What makes Williams’ decision striking is the public support she expressed for the people serving under her. Speaking to FOX23, she praised the volunteers’ training, their time away from family and their efforts to meet the standards she established. She said inadequate communication and daytime staffing contributed to the resignations. Those are her explanations for the dispute.

The council’s agenda had included possible termination of Williams and two police leaders. Town attorney Ben Sherrer said those items could not proceed because the employees had not received proper notice, KJRH reported. Firefighters nevertheless resigned during the meeting, followed by Williams.

Her willingness to leave her position alongside her crew deserves recognition. Supporting firefighters means listening to their concerns and acknowledging the commitment required to answer emergency calls. Williams made that support unmistakably public.

That solidarity also came with consequences for the community. The departures reduced available staffing, and Williams warned that neighboring departments would face additional demands through mutual aid. KJRH noted that Mayor Brenda Cunningham had previously defended the town’s management of the department.

By October 15, town officials were working to restore staffing. FOX23 reporting published by KRMG said the board had introduced new personnel and appointed Kirk Kvittum interim chief, effective November 1. Kvittum assured residents that emergency responses would continue.

The available reporting leaves questions about the full history of the dispute. But Williams’ public stand is clear: when her volunteers decided they could no longer continue, their chief chose to share the consequences.

That is a powerful expression of loyalty—and a reminder that the relationship between a fire chief and the people answering the calls deserves the attention of every community they protect.

Analysis based on reporting by KJRH/2 News Oklahoma and FOX23 News, as published by KRMG.
